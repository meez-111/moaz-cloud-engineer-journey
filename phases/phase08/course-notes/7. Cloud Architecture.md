## 1. What is a Solution Architect?

A **Solution Architect** is a professional responsible for designing technology solutions that meet specific business requirements. They bridge the gap between business needs and technical implementation.

**Key responsibilities:**

| Responsibility | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Requirement gathering** | Understand business goals and translate them into technical requirements. |
| **Design** | Create architectural diagrams, choose appropriate technologies, define integration points. |
| **Trade‑off analysis** | Balance security, cost, performance, reliability, and operational complexity. |
| **Documentation** | Produce clear architectural documents and diagrams for stakeholders. |
| **Communication** | Explain technical decisions to non‑technical stakeholders (executives, product managers). |
| **Delivery** | Guide the development team through implementation. |

**Analogy:** A solution architect is like the chief architect of a building – they design the blueprint, choose materials, and ensure the building is safe, functional, and within budget.

---

## 2. What is a Cloud Architect?

A **Cloud Architect** is a specialised type of solution architect who focuses on **cloud‑based** solutions. They design architectures that leverage cloud services (AWS, Azure, GCP) to meet business needs.

**Key skills of a cloud architect:**

| Skill | Why It Matters |
|-------|----------------|
| **Cloud services knowledge** | Understand what each cloud service does (EC2, S3, Lambda, RDS, etc.). |
| **Security** | Design secure architectures (IAM, encryption, VPC, WAF, Shield). |
| **Cost optimisation** | Minimise cloud costs (right‑sizing, Reserved Instances, Spot instances). |
| **Scalability** | Design systems that can handle growth (Auto Scaling, load balancing). |
| **Reliability** | Ensure high availability and fault tolerance (Multi‑AZ, Multi‑Region). |
| **Automation** | Use Infrastructure as Code (IaC) – CloudFormation, Terraform. |

**Cloud architect vs. Solution architect:** A cloud architect is a solution architect who specialises in cloud platforms. The terms are often used interchangeably in the cloud context.

## 3. Business Factors Every Architect Must Consider

A solution architect must balance multiple business factors when designing a solution. The two most important are:

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Security** | How secure is the solution? Does it meet compliance requirements? (e.g., GDPR, PDPL, HIPAA). |
| **Cost** | How much will this solution cost? What is the total cost of ownership (TCO)? |

**Other factors:**

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Performance** | Does the solution meet response time and throughput requirements? |
| **Reliability** | Is the solution highly available and fault‑tolerant? |
| **Operational excellence** | Can the solution be managed, monitored, and updated easily? |
| **Sustainability** | What is the environmental impact? |

**Trade‑off example:** You can achieve higher availability by deploying across multiple Regions, but that increases cost. You must decide which trade‑off is acceptable for the business.

## 4. Key Architecture Terminologies

### 4.1 Availability

**Definition:** The percentage of time a system is operational and accessible.

| Availability % | Downtime per Year | Downtime per Month | Downtime per Week |
|----------------|-------------------|--------------------|-------------------|
| 99% (2 nines) | 3.65 days | 7.31 hours | 1.68 hours |
| 99.9% (3 nines) | 8.77 hours | 43.83 minutes | 10.08 minutes |
| 99.99% (4 nines) | 52.6 minutes | 4.38 minutes | 1.01 minutes |
| 99.999% (5 nines) | 5.26 minutes | 26.3 seconds | 6.05 seconds |

**AWS SLA example:** Amazon RDS offers a 99.95% availability SLA for Multi‑AZ deployments.

### 4.2 Scalability

**Definition:** The ability to increase or decrease capacity **manually** as needed.

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Vertical scaling** | Increase the size of an existing resource (e.g., `t2.micro` → `t2.large`). |
| **Horizontal scaling** | Add more resources (e.g., add more EC2 instances). |

**Cloud benefit:** You can scale without physical hardware procurement.

### 4.3 Elasticity

**Definition:** The ability to **automatically** scale capacity based on real‑time demand.

**Example:** An Auto Scaling Group adds instances when CPU usage exceeds 70% and removes them when it drops.

**Scalability vs. Elasticity:**
- **Scalability** – manual, you decide when to scale.
- **Elasticity** – automatic, the system scales based on metrics.

### 4.4 Fault Tolerance

**Definition:** The ability of a system to continue operating even when one or more components fail.

**AWS mechanisms:**
- **Multi‑AZ** – deploy across multiple Availability Zones.
- **Load balancing** – distribute traffic to healthy instances.
- **Health checks** – detect failures and replace instances.
- **Redundancy** – have backup components ready to take over.

**Example:** If an EC2 instance fails, the load balancer stops sending traffic to it, and the Auto Scaling group launches a new instance.

### 4.5 Durability

**Definition:** The ability to preserve data without loss over time.

| Service | Durability |
|---------|------------|
| **S3 Standard** | 99.999999999% (11 nines) |
| **S3 Glacier** | 99.999999999% (11 nines) |
| **EBS volumes** | 99.999% (5 nines) |

**Why durability matters:** If you store data in S3, it is automatically replicated across multiple devices and AZs. Even if one device fails, your data is safe.

### 4.6 Disaster Recovery (DR)

**Definition:** The process of restoring systems and data after a catastrophic event (e.g., natural disaster, cyberattack, human error).

**DR objectives:**
- **RTO (Recovery Time Objective)** – How quickly you need to recover (time).
- **RPO (Recovery Point Objective)** – How much data you can afford to lose (data loss).

## 5. High Availability (HA)

**Definition:** A system design that ensures a high level of operational uptime, minimising downtime.

**AWS HA mechanisms:**

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Multi‑AZ** | Deploy across at least two Availability Zones. |
| **Auto Scaling** | Automatically replace failed instances. |
| **Load balancing** | Distribute traffic across healthy instances. |
| **Health checks** | Detect failures and route traffic away. |
| **RDS Multi‑AZ** | Synchronous replication to a standby instance in another AZ. |
| **Global services** | Use CloudFront, Route 53, and Global Accelerator to improve availability. |

**Architecture diagram:**

## 6. High Scalability

**Definition:** The ability to increase capacity **manually** when demand grows.

**Vertical scaling:**
```
t2.micro → t2.medium → t2.large
(1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM) → (2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM) → (4 vCPU, 8 GB RAM)
```

**Horizontal scaling:**

```
Before: 1 EC2 instance
After:  5 EC2 instances (behind a load balancer)
```

**AWS services for scaling:**
- **EC2 Auto Scaling** – manual or automatic scaling.
- **RDS** – scale read capacity with read replicas.
- **DynamoDB** – auto‑scaling for read/write capacity.

---

## 7. High Elasticity

**Definition:** The ability to **automatically** scale capacity based on real‑time demand.

**Auto Scaling Group example:**

```
Metric: CPU usage > 70%
Action: Add 2 more EC2 instances

Metric: CPU usage < 30%
Action: Remove 2 EC2 instances
```

**Benefits of elasticity:**
- You pay only for what you use.
- Handle traffic spikes without manual intervention.
- No over‑provisioning or under‑provisioning.

---

## 8. Fault Tolerance

**Definition:** The ability to continue operating despite failures.

**AWS fault‑tolerant design patterns:**

| Pattern | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Multi‑AZ** | Deploy across AZs. |
| **Redundancy** | Have multiple instances, load balancers, and replicated storage. |
| **Health checks** | Detect failures and route traffic away from unhealthy instances. |
| **Auto Scaling** | Replace failed instances automatically. |

**Example:** If one AZ goes down, the load balancer routes traffic to the other AZs. The Auto Scaling group launches new instances in the healthy AZs.

---

## 9. High Durability

**Definition:** The ability to preserve data without loss over time.

**AWS durability examples:**
- **S3 Standard:** 11 nines (99.999999999%) durability.
- **S3 Glacier:** 11 nines durability.
- **EBS:** 99.999% durability (5 nines).

**What 11 nines means:** If you store 10,000 objects in S3, you can expect to lose one object every 10,000,000 years.

## 10. Business Continuity Plan (BCP)

**Definition:** A plan that outlines how a business will continue operating during and after a disruptive event.

**BCP components:**

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Risk assessment** | Identify potential risks (natural disasters, cyberattacks, human error). |
| **Business impact analysis** | Determine the impact of each risk on business operations. |
| **Recovery strategies** | Define how to recover (DR options, RTO, RPO). |
| **Plan documentation** | Write down the plan, roles, responsibilities, and procedures. |
| **Testing** | Regularly test the plan (DR drills). |
| **Review and update** | Keep the plan current as the business and technology change. |

**Cloud's role in BCP:** Cloud makes DR easier and cheaper – you can replicate data and applications across Regions.

## 11. Disaster Recovery Options

| Option | Description | RTO | RPO | Cost |
|--------|-------------|-----|-----|------|
| **Backup and Restore** | Back up data to S3; restore when needed. | High (hours) | High (hours) | Lowest |
| **Pilot Light** | Keep a minimal version of your infrastructure running. | Medium (minutes) | Low (minutes) | Low |
| **Warm Standby** | Keep a scaled‑down version of your application running. | Low (minutes) | Low (minutes) | Medium |
| **Multi‑Site Active/Active** | Run the application in multiple Regions simultaneously. | Very low (seconds) | Very low (seconds) | Highest |

**AWS DR services:**
- **S3 Cross‑Region Replication (CRR)** – replicate data across Regions.
- **RDS Multi‑AZ** – synchronous replication to a standby instance in another AZ.
- **CloudEndure Disaster Recovery** – AWS service for migrating and recovering workloads.
- **AWS Backup** – centralised backup management.

## 12. RTO (Recovery Time Objective) Visualised

**RTO** is the maximum acceptable time between a disaster and the restoration of services.

```
Disaster Occurs        RTO (e.g., 4 hours)      Service Restored
        │                         │                     │
        ▼                         ▼                     ▼
    ─────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┼──────────▶ Time
        │                         │                     │
        │      Business Impact    │                     │
        │      (Downtime)         │                     │
```

**AWS strategies for low RTO:**
- Use **Auto Scaling** to automatically replace failed instances.
- Use **Multi‑AZ** to keep a standby copy of your database.
- Use **Global Accelerator** to quickly reroute traffic.

---

## 13. RPO (Recovery Point Objective) Visualised

**RPO** is the maximum acceptable data loss measured in time.

```
Disaster Occurs        RPO (e.g., 1 hour)      Last Backup / Replication
        │                         │                     │
        ▼                         ▼                     ▼
    ─────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┼──────────▶ Time
        │                         │                     │
        │      Data Loss (1 hour) │                     │
        │                         │                     │
```

**AWS strategies for low RPO:**
- Use **S3 Cross‑Region Replication** for near‑real‑time replication.
- Use **RDS Multi‑AZ** for synchronous replication (RPO = 0).
- Use **DynamoDB Global Tables** for multi‑Region replication.

## 15. Quick Reference Table

| Term | Definition | AWS Example |
|------|------------|-------------|
| **High Availability** | System remains operational most of the time. | Multi‑AZ, ALB, Auto Scaling |
| **Scalability** | Manually increase/decrease capacity. | Change instance type, add instances |
| **Elasticity** | Automatically scale based on demand. | Auto Scaling Groups |
| **Fault Tolerance** | Continue operating despite failures. | Multi‑AZ, health checks, redundancy |

| **Disaster Recovery** | Restore after catastrophic event. | Cross‑Region Replication, RDS Multi‑AZ |
| **RTO** | Max acceptable downtime. | Auto Scaling, Multi‑AZ |
| **RPO** | Max acceptable data loss. | Replication, synchronous writes |

## 16. Practice Lab – Verify Your Understanding

1. **Scalability vs. Elasticity:**  
   You manually change your EC2 instance from `t2.micro` to `t3.medium`. Is this scalability or elasticity? (Scalability – manual.)

2. **Fault tolerance:**  
   You deploy your application across three Availability Zones. What benefit does this provide? (Fault tolerance – if one AZ fails, the others remain operational.)

3. **Durability:**  
   Which AWS service has 11 nines of durability? (S3.)

4. **RTO vs. RPO:**  
   Your application requires a maximum downtime of 1 hour and a maximum data loss of 15 minutes. What are the RTO and RPO? (RTO = 1 hour, RPO = 15 minutes.)

5. **DR strategy:**  
   You replicate your S3 bucket to another Region. What DR strategy is this? (Backup and Restore – or Cross‑Region Replication.)

6. **Architecture design:**  
   You are building a web application that must be highly available and fault‑tolerant. What AWS services would you use? (ALB, Auto Scaling, Multi‑AZ RDS, S3, CloudFront.)
